# Content validation: is each lesson useful?

**Date:** 2026-11-01. **Status:** framework defined; **desk review done** (by the product team / AI assistant, not by learners); **field validation not done.**

Every rating below is a **design judgement** based on:
- the PRD personas and problem statement
- the existing item bank and lesson content
- common microfinance and financial-literacy practice

**None of it is evidence from learners or coordinators.** Section 4 lists what has and has not been validated.

## 1. Framework

Each lesson is rated on five questions, from 1 (weak) to 3 (strong).

| # | Question | 3 = strong | 1 = weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| R | **Relevant to a real business decision?** | Changes a decision she makes daily or weekly (how much stock to buy, what she can take home) | Abstract or rare |
| S | **Easy to explain with a relatable scenario?** | One scenario from her own stall, ≤ 3 numbers | Needs several steps, ideas or long text |
| M | **Important enough for MVP?** | Others depend on it, or a common costly mistake | Nice to have |
| A | **Can she apply it after the lesson?** | A concrete action at the stall this week, no new tools | Needs a bank, paperwork or a big change |
| C | **Can a coordinator reinforce it at the next visit?** | A 2-minute check-in question with a visible result | Hard to observe or to discuss without private numbers |

**Classification:**

| Class | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Strong MVP lesson** | Total ≥ 13 and no score of 1 |
| **Good but later** | Useful (total ≥ 10) but depends on another lesson, or is socially or behaviourally hard to act on yet |
| **Too complex for now** | S or A = 1 |
| **Needs redesign** | The idea is valuable, but the current content mixes concepts or drifts out of scope (e.g. product advice) |

**Evidence levels used below:**

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| D | Desk review only (this document) |
| A | Supported by app data (e.g. diagnostic correct rates, challenge check-ins) |
| F | Field-validated (learner and coordinator testing) |

**Every lesson is at level D today.**

## 2. Review of the five lessons

### 2.1 Sales and profit ("what is really left for you")

| | |
|---|---|
| **Why it matters** | The PRD's first question, "Did I make money this week?", cannot be answered without it. Counting all sales money as profit leads to overspending the cash box and to under-buying stock the next morning. Every other topic builds on "money in minus money out" |
| **Practical application** | Tonight: count the day's sales money, take away what she paid at the mandi this morning, and see what is really left. The app's weekly task asks her to do this on 3 evenings |
| **Difficulty** | Foundational (one subtraction). Applied version: several costs |
| **Ratings** | R 3 · S 3 · M 3 · A 3 · C 3 = **15** |
| **Content status** | Full lesson in the app (en / hi / te). Easy + harder practice. 3 weekly tasks. Hindi and Telugu not native-reviewed |
| **Classification** | ✅ **Strong MVP lesson** |

### 2.2 Everyday costs (small and hidden costs: spoilage, transport, gas, bags)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Why it matters** | Lakshmi (persona) throws away 10–15% of her leafy vegetables and does not count it. Small costs are why "profit" feels bigger than the money in the box. It is the natural second step after 2.1, using the same arithmetic with more costs. It also covers the household-vs-business expense distinction (school fees are not a shop cost) |
| **Practical application** | For one week, put spoiled vegetables in one bag and count what they cost. Add the auto fare to the stock cost when deciding prices. App weekly task: "Find one cost you usually forget and tell your coach" |
| **Difficulty** | Foundational: "Which is a business expense?". Applied: stock + transport = total cost |
| **Ratings** | R 3 · S 3 · M 3 · A 3 · C 3 = **15** |
| **Content status** | Diagnostic items (A + B) and a weekly task exist. **No in-app lesson yet.** Lesson 1's practice already introduces spoiled goods |
| **Classification** | ✅ **Strong MVP lesson** (next one to write) |

### 2.3 Two pockets: home money vs shop money

| | |
|---|---|
| **Why it matters** | Sunita (persona) takes household money from the cash box daily. Without separation she cannot see whether the shop makes money, and tomorrow's stock money disappears. It is practical, needs no bank, and coordinators already recognise "two tins" advice |
| **Practical application** | Keep two tins or pouches for 3 days; take a fixed amount for home instead of "whenever needed". App weekly tasks: "two places for 3 days" and an easier "for one day" |
| **Difficulty** | Foundational idea ("record the ₹150 taken for food"). Applied: "home money should come from a fixed amount" |
| **Ratings** | R 3 · S 3 · M 3 · A 2 · C 3 = **14** |
| **Why A = 2** | The action depends on family dynamics: a husband may take from the box. That is real and sensitive. The lesson must offer small steps and never shame her |
| **Content status** | Diagnostic items and 2 weekly tasks exist. **No in-app lesson yet** |
| **Classification** | ✅ **Strong MVP lesson.** Absorb "keep tomorrow's stock money" from 2.5 here |

### 2.4 Customer credit (udhaar)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Why it matters** | Farzana (persona) has a large unpaid credit book. Credit sales are not cash in the box, so "sales were fine but I have no money". Very relevant for kirana and tea stalls |
| **Practical application** | Foundational: know that today's udhaar is not today's cash. The useful *decisions* (a credit limit per customer, chasing repayment, refusing a neighbour) are social and need more than one lesson. Writing down who owes what drifts toward **bookkeeping**, which is out of scope |
| **Difficulty** | Foundational: is udhaar cash? Applied: cash in the box = cash sales only. Real behaviour change: high |
| **Ratings** | R 3 · S 2 · M 2 · A 1 · C 2 = **10** |
| **Content status** | Diagnostic items only; no weekly task; no lesson |
| **Classification** | 🕓 **Good but later.** Teach the foundational idea after 2.1–2.3. Design the action part with coordinators, without bookkeeping, before building it |

### 2.5 Saving a little daily

| | |
|---|---|
| **Why it matters** | A buffer protects the business from shocks. **But** the current content mixes two different ideas: "don't spend tomorrow's stock money" (sav_a: cash management, very useful) and "where is it safer to keep savings — box or bank account" (sav_b). The second edges toward savings-product advice, which the app must not give (PRD non-goal; safety guards redirect such questions to the coach) |
| **Practical application** | Daily saving is a habit, so it is better coached over time than taught in one lesson. "Keep ₹600 for tomorrow's stock" is immediately actionable and fits *Two pockets* |
| **Difficulty** | Mixed |
| **Ratings** | R 3 · S 2 · M 2 · A 2 · C 2 = **11**; drifts out of scope |
| **Content status** | Diagnostic items only |
| **Classification** | 🔧 **Needs redesign.** Move "keep tomorrow's stock money" into Two pockets. Later, rebuild "saving a little" as a habit lesson that names no products or institutions |

## 3. Built-in usefulness signals (in the app today)

The app already collects data that can **test** usefulness once real learners use it. No real data exists yet; the demo learners are fictional.

| Signal | Where | Read it as |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly-task check-in: done / partly / not yet + the difficulty she names | Check-in screen → Coach View | **Applicability (A):** a lesson whose tasks are mostly "not yet: hard to count" is not actionable as designed |
| Lesson result: understood / developing / needs review | Lesson completion → Coach View | **Explainability (S)** |
| Diagnostic per-item correct rates, foundational vs applied | Assessment records | **Difficulty calibration** (DIAGNOSTIC_STRATEGY.md §7) |
| Misconceptions resolved after the lesson | My learning, Coach View | **Learning gain** |
| Coach overrides with reasons ("learner asked for this topic", "fits her business right now", "already knows the suggested topic") | Coach View log | **Relevance (R)** as judged by the coordinator |

## 5. Field validation plan (before scaling content)

1. **Coordinator card-sort (5–8 coordinators, 1 hour).** For each topic, rate R / S / M / A / C (1–3) and name the 3 topics they would teach first. Compare with §2.
2. **Learner scenario check (8–10 learners per language).** Play each lesson's opening scenario and ask:
   - "Does this happen at your stall?"
   - "What would you do?"

   Record: recognised yes/no, and the decision she describes.
3. **Pilot metrics (4 weeks):**
   - weekly-task "done" or "partly" rate per lesson (target ≥ 50%; a guess to revisit)
   - misconceptions resolved after the lesson
   - coach overrides away from a topic ("already knows" / "fits her business" for another topic)
4. **Decision rule.** A lesson stays in the MVP if coordinators rate it ≥ 2 on R and A, and ≥ 40% of learners recognise its scenario. Otherwise redesign it. These thresholds are also assumptions; agree them with the NGO.
